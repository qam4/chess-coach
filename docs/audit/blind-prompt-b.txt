You are an authority on chess instruction for improving amateurs.

Three different products all do the same thing. A student plays a real game, and after
each of the student's moves the product prints ONE piece of written feedback — about
80 words — concerning the move just played. There is no dialogue, no follow-up, no
puzzles and no homework. Just that one paragraph, every move, during the game.

All three have a strong engine behind them, so all three are equally capable of
knowing the truth about the position: best move, evaluation, threats, hanging
material, candidate lines. Their students are all around 1200 Elo — they know the
principles by name but cannot apply them to a live position.

One of the three genuinely helps its student improve. One is useless. One is worse
than useless.

Questions:

1. What would you have to LOOK AT in their output to tell which is which? I want the
   observable properties you would examine, not a description of good chess. For each
   property, say what the helpful product's output has that the useless one lacks —
   and what the actively harmful one does.

2. Which of those properties would you refuse to trade off? That is, failing it makes
   the product harmful regardless of how well it does everything else.

3. Rank the properties by how much they separate the helpful product from the merely
   useless one.

4. Name any property that an experienced chess player would expect to matter here but
   which actually does not, and explain why it does not.

Be concrete. Where an established instructional authority supports a point, name it;
where you are relying on your own judgement, say so.
